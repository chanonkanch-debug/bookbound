# Lore: The Unwritten

Draft for the owner's approval (The Unwritten, Phase 2, step 1). Nothing here is built yet.
Once approved, the player-facing text goes into the data layer and this file becomes the
reference the later phases follow.

The rule for everything below: **the game never explains this.** The player pieces it
together from fragments, the journal, the Hearth's people and what the world does. This
file is the truth the writers know; players only get glimpses of it.

---

## 1. The world in five sentences

1. The world was written into one great book, and for a long time the book was full.
2. Every person is a page torn from it. That is why your book is you and can never be
   swapped, and why nobody chooses theirs.
3. Something is unwriting the world from its edges inward. It has no face, only pale blank
   paper where lines used to be. The people of the Hearth call it **the Eraser**.
4. Ink monsters are what it leaves behind: half-erased ink that still moves, still trying
   to be the words it was.
5. The Hearth is the spine of the book, the place where the binding still holds, and the
   one place the pale has never reached.

## 2. Where this meets the game as it is

The seed said every person is *born holding a book*. In the game you start bare-handed and
receive your book at the Ceremony at level 5. So:

- **You are born holding your page, not your book.** A page is small, folded, and mostly
  blank. It lives in your hands, which is why your hands learn, remember and take on a
  colour before you ever hold a book.
- **The Ceremony is when your page has been written enough to be bound.** The Hearth binds
  it into a book. That's why the book "seems to know you already": it is made of what you
  did bare-handed. Your leaning tilts the book because the book is literally your fighting
  written down.
- **Resting is writing.** Levels are only written when you rest, because resting is when you
  sit by a fire and write yourself down. Restoring an erasure site works the same way: you
  rest at its centre and write the place back. One action, one meaning, everywhere.
- **Thickness is how much of the great book your page came from.** A thin book is a single
  page with a lot written on it; a thick book is a bundle of loose leaves, each holding less.
  No one is more of a person than anyone else. (This is the sidegrade rule, told as lore.)
- **Elements are the inks the great book was written in.** Fire for what burns to be said,
  water for what flows between things, earth for what must stay, wind for what moves on,
  lightning for what connects. These match the verbs the game already uses (Escalate,
  Redirect, Persist, Move, Chain), so the lore never fights the design.
- **Hidden chapters are lost margins of the great book**: notes the first writers left in
  the margins, which wake only when someone lives them out. Your page carries one margin
  from wherever it was torn. You never learn whose note it was.
- **Mira** is another page, older than you, who has already been bound. She doesn't talk
  about where her page came from. (Room for Phase 8, where other mages show that everyone
  carries a margin.)
- **Death** is the pale touching you: "Something was taken from me." Book wear in Phase 4
  (smudges, tears) is the same idea made visible.

## 3. The monsters

All three are erased writing that hasn't stopped moving. None are evil; they're lost.

| monster | what it was | why it behaves as it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Inkling** | letters left behind when a word is rubbed out | they come in twos because no letter likes to be alone; they still try to spell something |
| **Blot** | ink spilled in a hurry, to cover something up | heavy and slow because it is hiding a great deal; it charges to cover whatever is in front of it |
| **Smudge Knight** | the old guards who stood at the margin's edge to hold back the pale, half-erased at their post | they never turn their backs, because they swore never to turn their backs to the white |

**Guardians** (Phase 2): at each erasure site, the pale has gathered smudges into something
larger. They aren't bosses, just tougher knots of the same lost ink.

| region | guardian | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| Inkling Fields | **the Scrawl** | a tangle of Inklings knotted into one restless line |
| Blot Marsh | **the Spill** | a Blot that has soaked up a whole pool |
| Knight's Reach | **the Hollow** | a Knight's armour with nothing written inside it |

## 4. The Eraser

What the writers know, and the player never fully gets:

The Eraser was once a person: a **Corrector**, one of the scribes who copied and kept the
great book. Copying carries mistakes forward, and the Corrector found one on the very first
page. Only one word. They took it out. But every line that leaned on that word was now wrong,
so they took those out too, and the lines that leaned on those, working outward from the
edges where the margins are thinnest. They could not stop. Somewhere along the way the
Corrector was erased by their own work, and only the erasing is left.

**What the word was is never said.** The fragments hint that the first word was a name.
They hint that it might have been the Corrector's own name, or the name of whoever wrote the
book. Was the Corrector mad, or right? The game never decides.

The second thread, also never resolved: the blank space isn't only loss. People are still
being born, holding new pages. Where does new paper come from, if the book was full? Perhaps
from the pale. Perhaps the Eraser is how the book makes room. The last fragment leaves both
readings open.

The boss in Phase 7 is this: a pale absence shaped by a habit of correcting. It doesn't
speak. Its "damage" is the blank cracking and ink showing through.

## 5. Places

| place | lore |
|---|---|
| **The Hearth** | the spine; the binding holds here, so the pale can't. Its fire is the only ink that never fades. Nothing follows you in. |
| **Inkling Fields** | the newest-written land, closest to the spine: grass, small things, mostly whole |
| **Blot Marsh** | where ink was spilled to hide something; soft ground, heavy things |
| **Knight's Reach** | the margin's edge, where the Knights held the line; the pale is strongest here |
| **Erasure sites** | places where the Eraser has passed: bare paper, thinning lines |
| **The Stacks** (Phase 7) | under the Hearth, where the Correctors kept the loose pages and their copies. Sealed since the Corrector went out to the edge. |

### Erasure sites (Phase 2)

Eight sites: two near the Hearth, three in the Marsh, three in the Reach (the pale is
stronger the further out you go). Each gets a short name the player only sees on the map
after the Cartographer (Phase 5), and a journal line when restored.

| region | site | restored, the journal says |
|---|---|---|
| Inkling Fields | the Bare Stile | "The fence came back, post by post, as if it had only been resting." |
| Inkling Fields | the Quiet Patch | "Grass grew back where there was none. I could hear it." |
| Blot Marsh | the Dry Pool | "Water came up through the paper and remembered where it had been." |
| Blot Marsh | the Sunk Path | "The stepping stones were there all along. Someone just had to write them." |
| Blot Marsh | the Reed Gap | "The reeds filled in, and something small started singing in them." |
| Knight's Reach | the Fallen Post | "A banner, still red. The Knights would have wanted it back." |
| Knight's Reach | the White Field | "The hill came back under my feet, all at once, like a held breath." |
| Knight's Reach | the Last Line | "There is a line cut in the stone here. Nobody crossed it while I stood on it." |

The first restore also writes: "I wrote it back. I didn't know I could do that."
Re-erasure, the first time a restored site starts to fade: "The pale is creeping back
where I haven't been. Places need to be remembered."

## 6. The fragments

Twelve torn pages, readable in any order. They aren't from the player's book: they're in
other hands, older than the Hearth. Each is a few lines, torn mid-sentence where it helps
(a long dash marks the tear). Shown in the Fragments section at the back of the book, as
torn paper, in the order found.

Where they lie (Phase 2 places nine; the last three wait for the Stacks in Phase 7):

| # | title (shown on the scrap) | where | whose hand |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A hymn | in the grass, Inkling Fields | a song everyone in the Hearth used to know |
| 2 | A rhyme | behind a stone, Inkling Fields | a child's skipping rhyme |
| 3 | The Hearth's ledger | the Bare Stile, once restored | a list of names |
| 4 | An oath | behind a stone, Knight's Reach | the Knights |
| 5 | On small things | in the grass, Blot Marsh | a Corrector's notes on Inklings |
| 6 | On spilled ink | the Dry Pool, once restored | the same notes, on Blots |
| 7 | What the Binder said | behind a stone, Blot Marsh | someone who knew the Binder |
| 8 | In the margin | the Fallen Post, once restored | a margin note |
| 9 | A letter, unsigned | the Last Line, once restored | the Corrector |
| 10 | A copyist's complaint | the Stacks, first floor (Phase 7) | a Corrector |
| 11 | A page, almost blank | the Stacks, second floor (Phase 7) | unknown |
| 12 | The last leaf | the Stacks, third floor (Phase 7) | unknown, or the Corrector |

### The texts

**1. A hymn**
> In the beginning was the page, and the page was full,
> and nothing was blank, and no one was alone,
> for every word leaned on the word before —

**2. A rhyme**
> Fold your page and keep it small,
> the white won't find you, not at all.
> Fold it once and fold it twice,
> hold it — —

**3. The Hearth's ledger**
> Tamsin, cooper. Old Bede. The twins from the far mill.
> (Three names crossed out with a pale line, not ink.)
> Ilse went out to the edge to look. Did not come back.
> Her page was found. It was blank.

**4. An oath**
> We stand at the last line of the margin.
> We do not turn our backs to the white.
> If it takes us, it takes us facing it.

**5. On small things**
> When a word is rubbed out, the letters are left. They don't know what they were.
> They go about in pairs, as if two of them might yet spell something.
> Be kind to them. — —

**6. On spilled ink**
> Someone spilled the marsh on purpose, to cover a line they didn't want read.
> The ink is heavy because of what it hides.
> I have not lifted it. I am afraid of what — —

**7. What the Binder said**
> She said a page can't be unbound from the book, only torn.
> And what is torn remembers where it was.
> That's why we write ourselves down at the fire. So we remember back.

**8. In the margin**
> Some passages wake only when they are lived.
> We left them in the margins for whoever came after,
> since we would not be there to — —

**9. A letter, unsigned**
> I only meant to make it right. You'd have done the same, if you had seen it there on the first page.
> One word. I took it out.
> But everything leaned on it. — — I can't stop. Every line I take leaves another one wrong.

**10. A copyist's complaint** (Phase 7)
> Every copy carries the mistakes of the last. Nobody reads the first page any more.
> I did. There is a word on it that shouldn't be there.
> They tell me to leave it. — —

**11. A page, almost blank** (Phase 7)
> (A whole page of pale paper. One sentence survives near the bottom.)
> The first word was a name.

**12. The last leaf** (Phase 7, found after the Eraser)
> If you are reading this, you were written after.
> So it didn't work.
> Or — —
> (The tear is clean, as if someone chose where to stop.)

### First fragment found

Journal: "A torn page, not from my book. The hand is older than the Hearth."
The first time the book opens to the Fragments section, its heading is simply **TORN PAGES**.

## 7. The voices

| who speaks | voice | example |
|---|---|---|
| the journal | you, first person, past tense, plain | "I wrote it back. I didn't know I could do that." |
| the book's margins | the book, to you, short and bare | "Be still. Then read." |
| fragments | other hands, older, in their own voice: hymn, rhyme, ledger, oath, notes, letter | see above |
| the Hearth's people (Phase 5) | one short line at a time, about the world, never about the rules | "The window's lit again. Haven't seen that since my mother." |

No voice ever uses game words (level, XP, damage, ink cost, cooldown) or explains a rule.
Fragments never mention the player's own hidden chapter.

## 8. What stays a mystery on purpose

Players can guess these, argue about them, and never be told:

- What the word on the first page was, and whose name.
- Whether the Corrector was right.
- Whether the pale is only loss, or also where new pages come from.
- Who wrote the great book in the first place.
- Where Mira's page was torn from.

## 9. Notes for the owner

- **Phase numbers in the brief.** The seed says the last fragments are in the Stacks
  "(Phase 6)" and restoring changes the Hearth "(see Phase 4)". In the phase list the Stacks
  are Phase 7 and the Hearth's people are Phase 5. I've followed the phase list.
- **The Binder** in Phase 5 is a living person in the Hearth who mends pages. Fragment 7
  talks about "the Binder" in the past tense, so the current Binder inherited the name. That
  gives Phase 5 a small thread to pull on, without anyone explaining it.
- **Ilse** (fragment 3) and the Corrector are deliberately left unconnected. Some players
  will connect them anyway.
- **"The white"** is what ordinary people call the pale; only the Hearth's elders say
  "the Eraser". Both appear, which helps it feel like folk knowledge, not a codex.
